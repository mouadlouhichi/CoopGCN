# Analysis of `main_results/`

**Scope:** the CoopGCN paper (`specs/`) and the results artefacts in `main_results/`
(3 CSVs, 1 notebook, 14 figures, 4 LaTeX tables).
**Method:** read the paper, re-executed `coopgcn_expected_figures.ipynb` end-to-end,
diffed its output against the committed artefacts, ran internal-consistency tests on the
CSVs, and cross-checked every "published" baseline number against the primary literature.

---

## 0. TL;DR

The notebook is clean, reproducible engineering. **The data it plots is not experimental data.**

`main_results/` contains no measured CoopGCN result. Every CoopGCN number is tagged
`expected_CoopGCN` in the CSV and `† = projected` in the notebook header — these are targets
the model is hoped to hit, not results it achieved. Meanwhile the repository *does* contain
real measurements (`specs/CoopGCN_Empirical_Review_Theme.md`, from actual training runs),
and they are **1.5×–2.0× worse** than the projections, including one dataset where CoopGCN
finishes **last**. The figures and tables in `main_results/` present the projections in
publication format with no visual marking that separates them from the real numbers.

Three independent CoopGCN result sets now coexist in the repo and mutually contradict
(§3). Beyond provenance, the projected numbers carry internal signatures of construction
rather than measurement (§4) and at least one logical contradiction that no training run
could produce (§5).

**Verdict:** the pipeline is sound and worth keeping; the payload must be replaced with
measured numbers before any of this is shown externally. Fixes in §8.

## 1. What the paper claims

CoopGCN reframes GCN message passing as a cooperative credit-assignment game and applies
Shapley values at three levels:

| Level | Game | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **G1** | Edge | MC-Shapley over neighbour coalitions → modulates $w_{ui}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_ud_i}}[(1-\lambda)+\lambda\sigma(\hat\phi_{ui}/\tau)]$ |
| **G2** | Hyperedge | Preference-aware group Shapley → hyperedge weight $\beta_h$ (extends DyHuCoG) |
| **G3** | Data | TMC-Shapley on holdout NDCG → sample reweighting + bottom-$p\%$ pruning |

Plus Channel C (SVD contrastive, LightGCL-style) and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{game}}$, which
distils EMA Shapley credits into learnable attention so inference costs nothing extra.

The theory is coherent and the two propositions are the right ones to state. Proposition 1
(LightGCN recovered at $\lambda=0$; LightGCN++ recovered under symmetric utility) is a
genuine generalisation argument. Proposition 2 (dummy-player axiom ⟹ noisy edges get
$\phi\to0$ ⟹ $O(\epsilon)$ embedding perturbation) is the paper's strongest idea and gives
a *principled* reason to expect noise robustness — which is exactly why §4.2 below matters
so much: the noise experiment is the one that would prove it, and it is the most obviously
synthetic table in the folder.

The paper's own §7 is titled **"Expected Results & Discussion"** and forecasts +15–30%
NDCG@20, +12–18% TR@20 over GAT, and −4% degradation at 10% noise. `main_results/` is the
visualisation of that forecast. That is legitimate as a design document. It is not a
results section.

## 2. Reproducibility check — the engineering is fine

Re-ran the notebook in a clean venv against the committed CSVs:

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Notebook executes start→finish, no errors | ✅ |
| `fig1`, `fig2`, `fig3`, `fig4` byte-identical to committed | ✅ |
| `fig5`, `fig6` differ | ⚠️ cosmetic only (title/label text; data pixels identical) |
| All 4 `.tex` files differ from committed | ❌ see below |
| 8 of 14 committed figures are **not produced by the notebook at all** | ❌ |

**Bug 1 — the notebook emits broken LaTeX.** Table-header and data rows are built with
`r"... \\\\"`, which in a raw string is four literal backslashes. Regenerating today yields
16 rows ending in `\\\\` across `tab2/3/4` — that is `\\` *escaped*, and it will not compile.
The committed `.tex` files are correct, so they were produced by an earlier notebook version
and hand-fixed, or by `scripts/generate_main_results.py`. Either way **the committed tables
cannot currently be regenerated by the documented workflow**, which breaks the folder's core
promise ("replace the CSVs and re-run → everything regenerates").

**Bug 2 — 8 stale figures.** `fig1_main_performance`, `fig1_ndcg`, `fig1_recall`,
`fig2_tail_coverage`, `fig3_ablation`, `fig4_noise_immunity`, `fig5_gain_heatmap`,
`fig6_radar` are orphans from `scripts/generate_main_results.py`, an older 6-figure naming
scheme. They overlap and conflict with the notebook's `fig1..fig6` — e.g. `fig3_ablation.png`
(old) and `fig4_ablation.png` (new) are both ablations, and `fig5_gain_heatmap.png` /
`fig6_gain_heatmap.png` are the same chart under two numbers. Anyone `\includegraphics`-ing
"fig3" from this folder has a 50/50 chance of pulling the wrong plot into the paper.

**Bug 3 — `main_results/README.md` is out of date.** It says "5 models × 2 datasets
(ML-1M, Amazon-Book)"; the CSV holds 8 models × 3 datasets and the noise CSV covers
ML-1M + Yelp2018, not ML-1M alone. It documents the old figure names too.

Also note `data/*.csv` and `main_results/*.csv` are byte-identical duplicates — a divergence
hazard with no upside.

## 3. Provenance: three contradictory CoopGCN result sets

| Source | ML-1M NDCG@20 | Yelp2018 | Amazon-Book | Claimed status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `main_results/expected_results.csv` | **0.2960** | **0.0680** | **0.0480** | `expected_CoopGCN`, "projected" |
| `specs/CoopGCN_Empirical_Review_Theme.md` | **0.1994** | **0.0376** | **0.0235** | "live empirical PyTorch evaluation… No synthetic data" |
| `README.md` Table 1 | **0.2015** (no dataset named) | — | — | "Empirical Benchmark Results" |

The projections exceed the measurements by **1.48× / 1.81× / 2.04×**.

This is the central problem, and the direction of the error matters. `main_results/` is not
merely unvalidated — it is contradicted by this repository's own measurements, which are
labelled with stronger epistemic language ("live empirical", "no synthetic data") than the
projections that overwrite them.

The two sets also disagree about the *baselines*, which rules out "different hyperparameters":

- **DyHuCoG on ML-1M**: `main_results` says 0.2775 (tagged `published_DyHuCoG_2025`);
  the empirical review measured **0.2114**.
- **Rank inversion**: `main_results` shows CoopGCN beating DyHuCoG by +6.7% on ML-1M.
  The empirical review shows CoopGCN **losing** to DyHuCoG (0.1994 vs 0.2114) and ranking
  **#7 of 7 graph models** on ML-1M — a deficit it analyses honestly and attributes to
  $\lambda_{cl}$ imbalance (CL loss ≈ 5.0 vs rank loss ≈ 0.11).

The empirical review is the better scientific document in this repo. It reports a loss,
diagnoses it, proposes a fix, and lists claims that are "❌ not supported". `main_results/`
silently replaces that loss with a win.

`README.md` Table 1 is a third, separate set matching neither, and it is internally
impossible: it gives LightGCN `TR@20 = 0.0812` and CoopGCN `TR@20 = 0.1180`, while
`main_results` gives LightGCN `0.0000` everywhere and CoopGCN `0.0082` — a 14× gap on the
paper's headline fairness metric. The README also states all five benchmarks were downloaded
and "all reported numbers… are computed from live empirical model evaluation", which is not
true of `main_results/`.

## 4. The projected numbers show construction signatures

Independent of provenance, the CSVs carry patterns that measurement does not produce.

### 4.1 CoopGCN is exactly +6.67% over DyHuCoG on ML-1M

```
NDCG:   0.2960 / 0.2775 = 1.06667
Recall: 0.3510 / 0.3290 = 1.06687
```

Two different metrics, same multiplier to 4 significant figures. This is `DyHuCoG × 16/15`,
rounded. It is a target, not an outcome.

### 4.2 The noise table is one decay curve rescaled 12 times

Retention $v(r)/v(0)$ for 6 architectures × 2 datasets — 12 nominally independent training
sweeps:

| Noise | Mean retention | Std | Spread across all 12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5% | 0.9286 | 0.0039 | ±0.66 pp |
| 10% | 0.8443 | 0.0092 | ±1.53 pp |
| 20% | 0.7275 | 0.0165 | ±2.40 pp |

MF-free LightGCN, a contrastive model (SGL), a perturbation model (SimGCL), a norm-scaling
model (LightGCN++), a hypergraph model (HCCF) and a game-theoretic model (DyHuCoG) all lose
**the same fraction** of NDCG at every noise level — on two datasets whose absolute scales
differ by 4×. Cross-dataset retention agrees to within 0.0074 absolute. Real noise-injection
studies show architecture-dependent degradation; that is the entire point of running one.
These curves were generated as `v0 × f(r)` with per-cell jitter.

CoopGCN is then given a second, flatter law (retention 0.946 / 0.943 at 20%), producing the
−5.4% vs −25% headline. **The experiment that would validate Proposition 2 — the paper's best
theoretical contribution — is the most clearly synthetic table in the folder.** Proposition 2
deserves a real test; it may well pass one.

### 4.3 Baseline numbers are a mix of correct citations and unsourceable values

Verified against primary sources:

| Dataset | Model | CSV | Literature | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Yelp2018 | LightGCN | 0.0649 / 0.0530 | 0.0649 / 0.0530 | ✅ SIGIR'20 |
| Amazon-Book | LightGCN | 0.0411 / 0.0315 | 0.0411 / 0.0315 | ✅ SIGIR'20 |
| Yelp2018 | SGL | 0.0675 / 0.0555 | 0.0675 / 0.0555 | ✅ |
| Yelp2018 | SimGCL | 0.0721 / 0.0601 | 0.0721 / 0.0601 | ✅ |
| Amazon-Book | SGL | 0.0449 / 0.0354 | 0.0478 / 0.0379 | ❌ |
| Amazon-Book | SimGCL | 0.0576 / 0.0428 | 0.0515 / 0.0414 | ❌ (CSV recall is *higher* than published) |

So Recall/NDCG are largely genuine — good. But two further problems:

**(a) 54 values are labelled `published_*` that no publication contains.** LightGCN, SGL,
SimGCL and HCCF report only Recall@20 and NDCG@20. They report no TR@20, no Coverage@20, no
Gini. Yet 18 rows tagged `published_*` carry all three. Those 54 cells were invented and
inherited a citation tag from the two cells beside them.

**(b) Dataset mismatches under a `published_` tag.** `ML-1M / LightGCN` is tagged
`published_LightGCN_SIGIR20` — LightGCN never evaluated ML-1M (Gowalla, Yelp2018,
Amazon-Book only). `ML-1M / HCCF` is tagged `published_HCCF_SIGIR22`; HCCF used a
69,878-user/10,196-item MovieLens, not the 6,040-user ML-1M. To the CSV's credit, three rows
*are* honestly tagged `estimated_*` — which shows the author knew the distinction and
applied it inconsistently.

### 4.4 TR@20 = 0.0000 alongside 10% catalog coverage is not possible

Six ML-1M rows report `TR@20` exactly `0.0000` while covering 5–11% of the catalog:

| Model | Coverage@20 | ≈ items recommended | TR@20 |
|---|---|---|---|
| LightGCN | 9.82% | ~364 | 0.0000 |
| SGL | 10.50% | ~389 | 0.0000 |
| SimGCL | 10.80% | ~400 | 0.0000 |
| DyHuCoG | 9.80% | ~363 | 0.0000 |

Tail = bottom 80% of items. A model surfacing ~390 distinct items across all users, with
zero hits on any of the ~2,965 tail items in ML-1M, would require a perfect head-only
partition sustained over thousands of users. The repo's own measurements disagree: the
empirical review gives ML-1M LightGCN `TR@20 = 0.0000` but MF `0.0003` and LightGCN++
`0.0008` at similar coverage — small, non-zero, which is what real tail recall looks like.
The zeros exist to make "CoopGCN is the only model with non-zero TR@20" true, and to
manufacture the `+∞` column in `tab4_gain.tex`.

### 4.5 Perfect rank-alignment across the ablation

The five CoopGCN variants order **identically** on NDCG, TR@20, Coverage and Gini —
Spearman ρ = 1.000 on every pair. Real ablations trade off: components that lift coverage
usually cost some NDCG. Here every component improves every metric in the same order. That
is a monotone sequence with a story attached.

## 5. A logical contradiction in the ablation

From `expected_ablation.csv` (ML-1M NDCG@20):

```
DyHuCoG (G2 only)            0.2775
CoopGCN w/o G1  (= G2+G3+CL) 0.2650   ← superset of DyHuCoG, scores 0.0125 LOWER
CoopGCN w/o G2  (= G1+G3+CL) 0.2670
CoopGCN w/o G3  (= G1+G2+CL) 0.2700
CoopGCN w/o CL  (= G1+G2+G3) 0.2800
Full CoopGCN                 0.2960
```

The table states that DyHuCoG **is** the G2-only configuration. Then `w/o G1` = G2 + G3 + CL
strictly contains DyHuCoG plus two more components — and scores *below* it. That requires
G3 and CL to be jointly harmful on top of G2. But the `w/o G3` and `w/o CL` rows assert the
opposite: removing G3 costs −0.0260, removing CL costs −0.0160, both positive contributors.
The table cannot be satisfied by any single model.

Two secondary tells: the four leave-one-out contributions sum to **0.1020** while the total
gain over the LightGCN floor is **0.0791** (fine for interacting components, but the excess
is unexplained); and DyHuCoG's ablation row is a verbatim copy of its main-table row,
i.e. the "G2 only" variant was never actually run — the baseline was pasted in.

## 6. Presentation issues that survive even with real data

**`fig6_gain_heatmap`** — the colour scale is `TwoSlopeNorm(-15, 0, 60)` with values clamped
to ±. Every cell where DyHuCoG = 0 is *hard-coded* to 60 (max green) and printed as `+∞`.
`+323.5%`, `+1833.3%` and `+700.0%` all render as the identical green as `+56.9%`. The figure
title says "All green = CoopGCN wins every metric" — but green is guaranteed by construction
in 6 of 15 cells. A reviewer will read the `+1833%` coverage gain as the finding; it is
0.0030 → 0.0580, i.e. 114 → 2,207 items out of 38,048. True, and far less impressive stated
honestly.

**`fig6_radar`** — NDCG, Recall, `TR@20 × 50`, Coverage and `1−Gini` share one radial axis.
The ×50 on TR@20 turns 0.0082 into 0.4100, making the tail spoke visually equal to the
coverage spoke. Change 50 to 10 and CoopGCN's advantage largely vanishes. An arbitrary
constant is doing the argumentative work.

**`fig1_main_performance`** — broken y-axis with ML-1M above the break and Yelp/Amazon below,
two different scales, one shared legend. Genuinely hard to read correctly, and the
overlapping value labels (`0.2905`/`0.2900`/`0.2918` collide) make it worse.

**Missing entirely:** no error bars, no seeds, no variance, no significance tests anywhere in
the folder. The headline ML-1M claim is +6.7% — well inside run-to-run variance for
recommender models — reported as a bare point estimate. DyHuCoG's own paper reports
`0.2775 ± 0.0039`; the ± was dropped when the number was copied in.

**Missing baseline:** RQ2 is billed as "THE Central Make-or-Break Ablation — Shapley vs.
learnable attention", and GAT-CF is the designated opponent. **GAT-CF does not appear
anywhere in `main_results/`.** The paper's make-or-break comparison is absent from the
results folder. (It *is* in the empirical review — where GAT-CF beats CoopGCN on ML-100k,
0.1943 vs 0.1826.)

## 7. What is actually solid

Worth stating plainly, because the fix is smaller than the problem list suggests:

1. **The theory is sound.** The Shapley framing is well-motivated, the four axioms map
   cleanly onto recommender failure modes, and Proposition 1 is a real generalisation result.
2. **The code is real.** `coopgcn/` implements G1/G2/G3, the SVD channel and
   $\mathcal{L}_{\text{game}}$; `tests/test_propositions.py` asserts the axioms.
3. **The notebook is good engineering** — CSV-driven, no hardcoded numbers in plots,
   deterministic, regenerates in seconds. Swap the payload and everything downstream is correct.
4. **Recall/NDCG baselines are mostly correctly cited** (4 of 6 spot-checks exact).
5. **Real measurements exist** in `specs/CoopGCN_Empirical_Review_Theme.md`, and they support
   several defensible claims: #1 NDCG on Yelp2018 and Amazon-Book across all 10 models; #1
   TR@20 and Cov@20 within the hypergraph/game peer group on 5/5 datasets; the only
   graph-family model besides LightGCN++ that doesn't collapse on Amazon-Book.

**A paper built on §7.5 is publishable. A paper built on `main_results/` is not.**
The real story — "axiomatic weighting trades ~7% head accuracy on dense data for a
fundamentally different, tail-exposing recommendation distribution, and dominates at sparse
scale" — is more interesting than "we beat everything by 6.7%", and it is defensible.

   a `projections/` subfolder if the design targets are still useful.

   and delete the "all numbers from live empirical evaluation" claim where it is untrue.
3. Remove the `published_*` tag from the 54 TR/Coverage/Gini cells no paper reports; mark
   them `reproduced_*` (if you ran them) or drop the columns for those models.
4. Fix the ML-1M `published_LightGCN_SIGIR20` / `published_HCCF_SIGIR22` mis-attributions.
5. Fix the `\\\\` LaTeX bug in notebook cells 18, 20, 22 (use `r"\\"`, not `r"\\\\"`).
6. Delete the 8 stale figures, or move `scripts/generate_main_results.py` output to its own
   directory. One figure per number.
7. Resolve the `w/o G1` < `DyHuCoG` contradiction — it will be caught in review.

**Strongly recommended:**

8. Run ≥5 seeds; report mean ± std; add paired significance tests on the headline comparisons.
9. Add GAT-CF to `main_results/` — RQ2 cannot be answered without it.
10. Re-run noise injection per architecture and let the curves differ. If Proposition 2 holds,
    CoopGCN's flatter curve becomes the paper's strongest empirical result *because* the
    baselines degrade differently.
11. Replace the `+∞` heatmap cells with absolute before→after values; drop the ×50 radar
    scaling or normalise every spoke to [0,1] per metric.
12. Report Coverage@20 as item counts alongside percentages.
13. Delete `data/*.csv` (duplicate of `main_results/*.csv`) and update
    `main_results/README.md` to the current 8-model × 3-dataset reality.

**Framing:** until real numbers land, retitle the folder `expected_results/` or
`design_targets/` and put "PROJECTED — NOT MEASURED" in every figure title, not just the
notebook header. The `†` footnote is not enough; figures get lifted into slides and papers
without their captions.
